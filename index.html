Thirty-Five Years In Business. Your Website Address Now Points To A Parking Page In Japan.

0
working web pages you own

You told Ruchi you have relied on word of mouth since you started. You have had no other option.

You have built a business since 1991 that covers three counties and does everything from a leaking tap to an extension. None of it is findable by anyone who does not already know you.

Domain, DNS and directory listing checked 24 September 2026
Finding 1 — Your Domain Has Been Taken By Somebody Else
www.ab-sessions-plumbing-building-services.com Loads over http yes — 1,665 bytes Loads over https times out completely Email records (MX) none Name servers dns1.onamae.com / dns2.onamae.com What is actually on it: "このドメインはお名前.comで取得されています" "This domain was acquired at Onamae.com" Copyright (c) 2025 GMO Internet, Inc.
In plain English

Your web address lapsed, and somebody in Japan registered it. Onamae.com is a large Japanese domain registrar and GMO Internet is the company behind it. What sits there now is their parking page.

It has no email on it either, so anything ever sent to an address on that domain has been going nowhere.

You cannot get it back by asking. It belongs to whoever registered it. The practical answer is a new address — a shorter one, which you want anyway. The current one is fifty characters long and nobody could ever say it down the phone.

Finding 2 — The Only Thing Google Can Find Is A Listing Written A Decade Ago
Your construction.co.uk listing says: "AB Sessions was founded by Adam Sessions in 1991" "With almost 25 years experience..." 1991 + 25 = 2016 So that description was written around ten years ago, and has not been touched since. It still points customers at: www.ab-sessions-plumbing-building-services.com (the parking page)
It is not wrong, it is just frozen

The listing itself is decent. It describes the plumbing, the building work, the maintenance, the 24-hour emergency cover and the no-call-out-charge policy. Somebody wrote it properly.

But it is the only description of your business on the internet, it undersells your experience by ten years, and the link on it is broken.

It also lists your address as Swandlincote. There is no such place — it is Swadlincote. Small thing, but it is the kind of thing that stops a listing matching a search.

Finding 3 — Your Whole Market Is Rented From Platforms
Search for a plumber in Swadlincote and what comes back: MyBuilder · Rated People · Checkatrade Houzz · BookaBuilder · StarOfService · 192.com Individual firms with their own strong website: very few
This cuts both ways, and the second half is the opportunity

The bad news: with no website of your own, the only routes to you are word of mouth and platforms. Platforms sell the same enquiry to several trades at once, take a fee, and own the customer relationship. You are renting access to people who are looking for exactly what you do.

The good news, and it is genuinely good: almost nobody in your patch has built anything either. This is not a market where you would be up against firms with fifty pages and a marketing department. The bar in Swadlincote is on the floor.

And you have the one thing none of them can buy: thirty-five years, and a customer list to prove it.

Finding 4 — The Range Is The Problem And The Opportunity
What you told Ruchi: Jobs range from £1,000–£3,000 to £20,000 Preference on size none Clients per week a couple What your listing says you do: leaking taps · burst pipes · full heating systems kitchens · bathrooms · tiling · decorating extensions · loft conversions · garage conversions hard landscaping · block paving · roof work · gutters
A tap and an extension are not the same business

Somebody looking for an emergency plumber and somebody planning a £20,000 extension are two completely different people, searching different words, on different timescales, with different worries. One wants you today. The other wants three quotes and six weeks to think.

A single website can serve both, but only if it is built to. One page saying “plumbing and building” serves neither of them well — which is roughly what a directory listing does today.

You said you have no preference on job size. That is fine, and it is unusual — but it means the website has to do the sorting, because right now your phone is doing it.

What We Would Do, In Order
  • Get a short domain you can say down the phone. The old one is gone and it was never usable anyway. This costs a few pounds a year.
  • Build the site — separate routes for emergency plumbing and for planned building work, because those are two different customers.
  • Put email on your own domain. A firm of thirty-five years should not be quoting a £20,000 extension from a free email address.
  • Claim and build a Google Business Profile, and ask every past customer for a review. You have thirty-five years of them and almost nothing published.
  • Correct the construction.co.uk listing — the spelling, the years, and the dead link. Free.
  • Then decide about the platforms. Once you have something of your own, the directories become a top-up rather than the only road in.

Thirty-five years, three counties, a couple of jobs a week and no way for a stranger to find you.

Nothing here is broken. It simply was never built.
